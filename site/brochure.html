Foundation
Life Insurance — More Than Just a Death Benefit

Most people think life insurance is just about leaving money behind when you die. That was true 30 years ago. Today's modern life insurance is one of the most powerful financial vehicles available — offering living benefits, tax-free growth, and retirement income — all in one policy.

Term Life Insurance

Pure protection for a set period — 10, 20, or 30 years. The most affordable way to get a large death benefit. Best for young families and income replacement.

Best for: Young parents, mortgage protection, replacing income. Premiums as low as $20/month for $500K coverage.

Whole Life Insurance

Permanent coverage that never expires, with guaranteed cash value that grows over time. Acts as a savings vehicle you can borrow against — tax-free.

Key feature: Guaranteed cash value growth. Your family is protected forever AND you build wealth you can use while you're alive.

Indexed Universal Life (IUL)

The most powerful modern policy. Growth is linked to a market index (like the S&P 500) — you participate in market gains but are protected from losses. Zero is your floor.

Powerful truth: IULs have historically outperformed 401(k)s on an after-tax basis — with zero market risk to your principal.

Final Expense Insurance

Smaller policies ($5K–$50K) designed specifically to cover end-of-life costs — funerals, medical bills, debts. No medical exam required. Approved in minutes.

Often overlooked: The average funeral costs $9,000–$12,000. Without coverage, that bill lands on your family the worst week of their lives.

Mortgage Protection

Specifically designed to pay off your mortgage if you die, become disabled, or face a critical illness. Keeps your family in their home — guaranteed.

Important: Your mortgage lender's life insurance protects the bank. This policy protects your family.

The Big Picture — Why Life Insurance First?

Before you invest a dollar, protect the income that generates those dollars. Life insurance is the foundation every financial plan should be built on. You insure your car. You insure your home. The most important asset — your income-generating ability — deserves the same protection.

The Feature Nobody Talks About
Living Benefits — Access Your Death Benefit While You're Alive

Living benefits are one of the most powerful and least understood features in modern life insurance. They allow you to access a portion of your death benefit while you're still alive if you experience a qualifying medical event. This changes everything.

A
Critical Illness Rider
Heart attack · stroke · cancer · major organ failure

If you're diagnosed with a qualifying critical illness, you can access a lump sum from your death benefit — typically 25–100% — to cover medical bills, lost income, or any expense you choose. No questions asked on how you spend it.

  • Receive up to 90% of your death benefit immediately upon diagnosis
  • Use funds for treatment, income replacement, or any purpose
  • Remaining benefit still pays upon death
B
Chronic Illness Rider
Unable to perform 2 of 6 Activities of Daily Living

If you become chronically ill and can no longer independently perform basic daily activities (bathing, eating, dressing, etc.), you can accelerate your death benefit to fund long-term care — without the cost of a separate LTC policy.

  • Replaces expensive standalone long-term care insurance
  • Access monthly benefit payments over time
  • No "use it or lose it" — unused benefit remains as death benefit
C
Terminal Illness Rider
Diagnosed with 12–24 months to live

If diagnosed with a terminal illness, access your death benefit early — typically 50–100% — to check off your bucket list, spend time with family, and handle your affairs on your own terms rather than leaving everything for your estate.

  • Live the life you want in your final months
  • Fund end-of-life care without burdening family
  • Often included at no additional cost

Why the IUL Wins on Taxes

A 401(k) gives you a tax break today — but the IRS collects later, at whatever the future tax rate is. An IUL grows completely tax-free and allows you to take income as tax-free policy loans. You never owe the IRS a single dollar on that growth. For most people, this is significantly more powerful over a 20–30 year horizon.

The Best Kept Secret in Finance
Roth IRA — Tax-Free for Life

If you qualify, a Roth IRA is one of the most powerful wealth-building tools available. You pay tax now — on today's (presumably lower) income — and never pay tax again on that money or its growth.

How It Works

Contribute after-tax dollars up to $7,000/year ($8,000 if 50+). That money grows tax-free in investments of your choice. At retirement, all withdrawals are completely tax-free — including all the growth.

Example: $7,000/year from age 25 to 65 at 7% average return = $1.5 million — completely tax-free at retirement.

Income Limits (2024)

You can contribute the full amount if your income is under $146,000 (single) or $230,000 (married). Phased out above those limits. Over the limit? A "Backdoor Roth" strategy still lets you contribute legally.

Backdoor Roth: Contribute to a Traditional IRA, then convert it to Roth. 100% legal strategy used by high earners.

5 Reasons to Choose Roth

  • Tax rates are likely to rise — lock in today's rate
  • No Required Minimum Distributions — ever
  • Access contributions penalty-free before retirement
  • Pass it to heirs completely tax-free
  • Does not count as taxable income in retirement
5
The Long Game
Retirement Planning — The 4 Questions You Must Answer

1. How Much Will You Need?

The 4% rule: Multiply your desired annual income by 25. Want $60,000/year? You need $1.5 million. Want $100,000/year? You need $2.5 million. Social Security covers a portion — how much do you need to generate yourself?

2. When Will You Retire?

Retiring at 60 vs 70 is a 10-year difference that dramatically changes your plan. Earlier retirement = more savings needed + longer time for it to last. Later retirement = fewer years of savings needed but more years working.

3. Where Will the Money Come From?

Most people have 3 potential sources: Social Security (40% of pre-retirement income on average), employer plans (401k/pension), and personal savings/investments. Relying only on one source is dangerous.

4. How Will You Protect It?

A 20-year retirement is a long time for markets to drop. Sequence of returns risk — a market crash early in retirement — can permanently devastate a portfolio. Protected vehicles like fixed and indexed annuities eliminate this risk.

Guaranteed Income for Life
Annuities & Private Pensions — Turn Savings Into a Paycheck

An annuity is essentially a private pension — you give an insurance company a lump sum, and they guarantee you income for life, no matter how long you live. It solves the #1 retirement fear: outliving your money.

Fixed Annuity

Guaranteed fixed interest rate (currently 5–6% in many products). Predictable, safe, and protected from market risk. The closest thing to a traditional pension available to individuals today.

Best for: Conservative investors who want predictable growth and guaranteed income without any market exposure.

Indexed Annuity

Growth linked to a market index (S&P 500, Dow Jones). Participate in market gains up to a cap rate (e.g., 10–12%), but your principal is protected — zero floor means you never lose to market downturns.

The power: Market-linked growth without market risk. When the index is up 20%, you might get 10%. When it drops 30%, you get 0% — not negative.

Income Rider — Your Personal Pension

An optional rider that guarantees a specific annual income for life — regardless of what happens to your account value. Even if your account hits zero, the income never stops. You cannot outlive it.

Example: Deposit $200,000 at age 55. At 65, receive $18,000–$24,000/year for life — guaranteed, no matter how long you live.

The Private Pension Strategy

Pensions have all but disappeared from the private sector. But you can build your own. By placing a portion of your savings into an indexed annuity with an income rider, you create guaranteed lifetime income — just like a pension. Many of our clients use this strategy to guarantee their essential expenses (housing, food, healthcare) are always covered, then use other assets for discretionary spending.

7
Don't Leave Money on the Table
401(k) Rollovers — Move Your Old Plans Before They Cost You

If you've left a job, you may have an old 401(k) sitting at your former employer — often in high-fee funds you didn't choose, with no ongoing management. Rolling it over to an IRA or annuity gives you more control, lower fees, and better options.

Why Roll Over?

  • Lower fees — employer plans often charge 1–2% annually vs. 0.1–0.5% for an IRA
  • More investment options — not limited to your employer's fund lineup
  • Consolidation — one account is easier to manage
  • Access to protected products (annuities) not available in 401(k)s
  • Better planning — your advisor can actually see and manage it

How It Works (Tax-Free)

A direct rollover (trustee-to-trustee transfer) moves your funds directly from your old plan to your new IRA or annuity with zero taxes and zero penalties. It's treated as a continuation of the same tax-deferred account.

Important: Never take a check yourself — always do a direct rollover. If you touch the money, you have 60 days to redeposit it or it becomes fully taxable.

The Fee Problem — Real Numbers

A 1% annual fee doesn't sound like much. Over 30 years on a $200,000 account growing at 7%, that 1% costs you over $180,000 in lost growth. Fee audits often reveal significant savings that can be reinvested into your own plan.
