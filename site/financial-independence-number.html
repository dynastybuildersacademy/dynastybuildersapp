Your Financial Independence Number

How to retire in 10 years or less — with more in your retirement account than you have today.

What Is a Financial Independence Number?
The amount that produces enough guaranteed and passive income to cover your living expenses — permanently — without touching your time for a paycheck.

Three Levers That Get You There Faster

Most people try to reach financial independence with only one lever: save more. These three, used together, are what actually compress the timeline.

1

Tax Efficiency

Money in the wrong tax bucket can lose 20–30% of its value to taxes before you ever spend it. Shifting new contributions toward tax-advantaged growth keeps more compounding for you.

2

Protected Growth

A single market downturn in the years right before retirement can permanently shrink your number. Vehicles with a 0% floor let growth compound without giving back the down years.

3

Guaranteed Income

Once income is guaranteed for life, the "number" you actually need gets smaller — you're no longer building a buffer against outliving your money.

The 10-Year Acceleration Plan

A simplified version of the sequence we walk through with clients working toward an accelerated timeline.

Yr 1–2
Calculate your real numberBased on your actual monthly expenses in retirement — not a generic rule of thumb — and audit where every dollar currently sits (tax-now, tax-later, tax-advantaged).
Yr 3–5
Redirect new contributionsNew savings get routed toward the mix of tax treatment and protection that closes your specific gap fastest, without disrupting what's already working.
Yr 6–8
Layer in guaranteed incomeAs the number gets closer, a portion shifts toward vehicles that can convert to income you can't outlive — reducing how large the number needs to be.
Yr 9–10
Lock in and de-riskThe final stretch shifts from growth-seeking to protecting what's been built, so a bad year right before retirement can't undo a decade of progress.

Mistakes That Add Years to Your Timeline

✕
Treating every dollar as the same dollarTax-deferred, tax-now, and tax-advantaged accounts each behave completely differently when you start drawing income — mixing them up costs years of extra work.
✕
Waiting for "someday" to run the numbersEvery year without a real number is a year of guessing whether you're actually on track.
✕
Full market exposure with no floor near the finish lineA downturn at the wrong moment can add 3–5 years to a timeline that was otherwise on track.
